# WAIS EXECUTION v1.2 — Public Architecture

Updated: 2026-08-12

This document is a sanitized public description of the WAIS execution process. Proprietary model weights, private portfolio data, credentials and internal decision rules must not be stored in the public repository.

## Why v1.2 changed

The previous workflow could leave a high-quality name in Candidate / Candidate+ too long and only use technical analysis as a final READY 1 confirmation. That creates a structural risk: by the time full confirmation arrives, a meaningful part of the move may already be gone.

WAIS EXECUTION v1.2 fixes this by making technical analysis continuous from Candidate onward and by inserting a separate TECH READY / Scout Entry stage before READY 1.

## Execution ladder

1. DISCOVERY / RESEARCH
2. CANDIDATE
3. CANDIDATE+
4. TECH READY / SCOUT ENTRY
5. READY 1
6. ADD / HOLD / PROTECT / EXIT

## Candidate and Candidate+

Technical evidence is already monitored here. WAIS does not wait for READY 1 before reviewing price structure, volume/momentum, moving-average repair/reclaim, relative strength, support, invalidation and event risk.

## TECH READY / Scout Entry

Purpose: permit a deliberately small evidence-based starter position before full READY 1 when the investment case is already strong and technical evidence is beginning to transition.

Public guardrails:

- Price alone cannot create Scout Entry.
- Fundamentals, catalysts and risk/reward must already be acceptable.
- Early transition evidence should be visible across multiple technical dimensions rather than one isolated indicator.
- The starter tranche must remain materially smaller than a normal READY 1 first tranche.
- Invalidation must be defined before entry.
- Failed transition returns the name to Candidate / Candidate+; it does not automatically justify averaging down.
- Earnings, macro events, liquidity and extended-hours quality can block or reduce Scout Entry.
- Thin pre-market or after-hours moves are supporting evidence, not equivalent to normal regular-session confirmation.

## READY 1

READY 1 remains the stronger first-tranche authorization. It requires alignment across price, fundamentals, catalysts, event risk and technical confirmation. TECH READY may happen earlier; READY 1 is not removed or weakened.

## Operating principle

The goal is not to buy earlier at any cost. The goal is to recognize an evidence-based transition earlier, participate with controlled risk when justified, and still preserve a higher-confidence READY 1 state for fuller deployment.
